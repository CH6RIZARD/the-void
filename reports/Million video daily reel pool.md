# Open protocols supply the pool, not a million

A million fresh, legally usable short videos per day is not reachable for The Void. Measured on 2026-10-11, every source an indie app can lawfully pull from adds up to roughly **110,000-130,000 fresh, directly playable video posts per day**. **Bluesky's Jetstream firehose supplies about 97,000 of them** and the Mastodon-led Fediverse about 13,000. Embed-only sources (YouTube, Twitch) can add a few thousand more candidates, but you have to play them in the platform's own player under strict rules. Once you keep only safe-for-work posts on The Void's themes (geopolitics, surveillance, resistance), the usable number per day is in the **low thousands to roughly 10,000**. That figure is an estimate built on filter rates nobody has measured yet. It is still enough. Feeds feel deep because users rarely see the same clip twice, not because the pool is huge. A heavy user watches about 200-300 clips a day, so a pool that adds a few thousand labelled clips per day and keeps them for 30-90 days (tens of thousands live at once) feels endless for a small audience. Recommended order: Bluesky Jetstream first, then Mastodon/fedi.buzz, a hand-picked set of PeerTube news and activism instances, and YouTube embeds from chosen channels. That stack runs for about **$40-70/month**. Skip Reddit, Telegram, X, TikTok, Instagram, Kick and Rumble.

## Bluesky alone delivers ~97,000 native videos a day for free

Bluesky is the only source that is high-volume, free, has no login and hands back a stream the app can play directly. A 90-second sample of Jetstream v2 (filtered to `app.bsky.feed.post`, taken on a quiet Sunday evening UTC) recorded 3,841 new posts. **100 of them carried an `app.bsky.embed.video` embed**, about 2.6%. Scaled up, that is **~3.7M posts and ~97,000 native video posts per day**, consistent with Bluesky's official figure of 1.41B posts in 2025 ([Jetstream docs](https://bsky.network/docs/jetstream/); [Bluesky transparency overview](https://bsky.social/about/blog/01-29-2026-transparency-report-overview)). The endpoint `wss://jetstream.us-west.bsky.network/xrpc/network.bsky.jetstream.subscribeEvents` needs **no authentication**, accepts up to 100 collections and 10,000 DIDs per subscription, and can resume from a cursor. Delivery is at-least-once, so writes should be keyed on the `at://` URI ([Jetstream docs](https://bsky.network/docs/jetstream/)). There is no server-side filter for embed type, so the proxy receives every post (about **3.7 GB/day of raw JSON**, ~110 GB/month) and drops the non-video ones locally. Bluesky's Jetstream author ran the public instance for 12 consumers on a **$5/mo VPS in under 16 MB of memory**, so CPU is not a concern ([jazco.dev](https://jazco.dev/2024/09/24/jetstream/)). The tail needs a long-lived WebSocket, so it belongs on the Node VPS, not on Cloudflare Workers.

The playback story is the best of any source. The raw post record holds a blob CID. From it you can build `https://video.bsky.app/watch/{did}/{cid}/playlist.m3u8`, the same URL Bluesky's own API returns. That is a public HLS master playlist with **360p and 720p** variants, served with `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` and 30-day public cache headers ([video lexicon](https://github.com/bluesky-social/atproto/blob/main/lexicons/app/bsky/embed/video.json); [Jetstream docs](https://bsky.network/docs/jetstream/)). hls.js on web and expo-video on iOS/Android play it without auth, so the proxy only serves JSON. The catch is a 720p ceiling: Bluesky clips look softer than native 1080p TikTok.

Two other Bluesky surfaces matter less. Community video feeds give you pre-curated clips. `getPopularFeedGenerators?query=video` works unauthenticated, and feeds such as SkyTok (~700 videos/day) and OG Videos+ (~330/day) returned 97-100% video posts without auth. Across the 10-15 good safe-for-work feeds that comes to **roughly 1,000-3,000 curated clips/day**. Bluesky's own "Video" feed returned errors without auth ([getPopularFeedGenerators](https://api.bsky.app/xrpc/app.bsky.unspecced.getPopularFeedGenerators?query=video&limit=100)). Search is a poor source. Unauthenticated `searchPosts` returned page one on `api.bsky.app` but blocked every later page with HTTP 403, and only **~3 in 100 results were videos**. The public cached host blocks search entirely, which other developers have reported too ([bsky-docs issue #332](https://github.com/bluesky-social/bsky-docs/issues/332)). A free app password (3,000 requests per 5 minutes per IP) is the fix if you want topic backfill ([rate-limits.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bluesky-social/bsky-docs/main/docs/advanced-guides/rate-limits.md)).

On terms, Bluesky expects third-party "Developer Applications" to display user content and has no clause against hotlinking ([Bluesky ToS](https://bsky.social/about/support/tos)). The Developer Guidelines impose duties instead. Apps must offer **reporting, content blocking and user blocking, must remove content users delete, and must publish a contact address**. Ignoring known violations can get you cut off from Bluesky's infrastructure ([Developer Guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bluesky-social/bsky-docs/main/docs/support/developer-guidelines.md)). Several of the most popular video feeds are adult, so filtering on content labels (`porn`, `sexual`, `graphic-media`, `!no-unauthenticated`) is required. Bluesky also calls Jetstream a tool for "casual, low-stakes projects" with no stability promise, so plan for it to change ([Bluesky Jetstream blog](https://docs.bsky.app/blog/jetstream)).

## The Fediverse adds ~13,000 videos a day, mostly from Mastodon

Most Fediverse video comes from Mastodon, not from the video-first platforms. fedi.buzz runs an **unauthenticated server-sent-events firehose** at `https://fedi.buzz/api/v1/streaming/public` that returns full Mastodon status JSON. A 60-second sample counted 416 statuses, of which 9 had video attachments. Scaled up, that is **~600,000 statuses and roughly 13,000 video posts per day**, most of them 10-40 seconds long ([fedi.buzz stream](https://fedi.buzz/api/v1/streaming/public)). One maintainer runs it with no documentation or terms, so treat it as a convenience. The software behind it (buzzrelay) is open source if you need to self-host later ([buzzrelay](https://github.com/astro/buzzrelay)). Mastodon media comes back as **direct MP4 files with CORS `*` and immutable caching** (fosstodon's CDN returned 206 with `Accept-Ranges: bytes`), so it plays natively ([fosstodon probe](https://fosstodon.org/api/v1/timelines/public?only_media=true&limit=40)). Access is getting tighter. **mastodon.social, mastodon.online, mstdn.social, infosec.exchange and chaos.social now return HTTP 422 for unauthenticated public timelines**, although mastodon.social still serves tag timelines (its `#video` tag returned 25 videos in a 7-hour window). Instances such as hachyderm.io, mastodon.world and universeodon.com stay open. The default limit is 300 requests per 5 minutes per IP ([Mastodon timelines docs](https://docs.joinmastodon.org/methods/timelines/); [rate limits](https://docs.joinmastodon.org/api/rate-limits/)). The polite minimum is to register an app token on each large instance and back off when you get 401 or 422.

PeerTube is the opposite: deep archive, little fresh content, but the content suits The Void best. The instance directory lists **1,785 instances and 947,322 videos but only ~1,574 daily active users** ([instances stats](https://instances.joinpeertube.org/api/v1/instances/stats)). Sepia Search, the cross-instance index, holds ~846,000 videos and is free and CORS-open. It adds only **~450 videos/day, 29% of them 90 seconds or shorter**. It lags about two weeks behind, and you cannot page past result 9,000 ([Sepia API](https://search.joinpeertube.org/api/v1/search/videos?count=1&sort=-publishedAt)). The News & Politics category adds ~40 videos/day across the federation and Activism ~5/day. About 20-25% of those are short, so **only 10-20 short news and activism clips per day** exist ([Sepia News & Politics](https://search.joinpeertube.org/api/v1/search/videos?categoryOneOf=11&startDate=2026-08-12T00:00:00.000Z&sort=-publishedAt&count=100&nsfw=false)). Each instance's API is unauthenticated, CORS-open and limited to 50 requests per 10 seconds. The video detail call returns an **HLS master playlist and progressive MP4 files** that kolektiva, framatube and tilvids all served with CORS `*` and support for range requests ([PeerTube REST docs](https://docs.joinpeertube.org/api-rest-reference.html); [kolektiva detail](https://kolektiva.media/api/v1/videos/6303dc21-e841-458b-927a-934dcde001ba)). Hosts worth polling directly include kolektiva.media, indymotion.fr, video.megaphone.news, video.europalestine.com, video.lemediatv.fr, tube.taz.de and tube.xrtv.nl. The Sepia archive also holds **196,000 videos of 90 seconds or less** for backfill. About 56% of recent uploads carry no licence ("Unknown"), so stream them from the origin with attribution and treat only CC BY, CC BY-SA and public-domain clips as clean.

Loops, the Fediverse's TikTok-style app, is not a source yet. The flagship instance has ~3,900 monthly active users and ~58,000 posts in total, and **every feed endpoint returns 401 or 404 without a token**. Its videos do reach Mastodon timelines through federation, so the fedi.buzz tail picks them up ([Loops nodeinfo](https://loops.video/nodeinfo/2.0); [Loops API docs](https://docs.joinloops.org/)). Pixelfed's public timeline also requires auth on pixelfed.social ([probe](https://pixelfed.social/api/v1/timelines/public?limit=5)).

## Big platforms are embed-only, expensive, or closing

Only Internet Archive, Odysee and, with terms-of-service risk, Reddit and Telegram return files your own player can use. Everything else forces you into its player or makes you pay.

| Source | Returns | Auth / limit | Display rules | Realistic fresh/day for The Void |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bluesky Jetstream | Direct HLS (360p/720p) | None | Report/block/delete duties, honour labels | ~97k raw video; low thousands on-theme |
| Bluesky video feeds | Direct HLS | None (most feeds) | Same | 1-3k curated |
| Mastodon / fedi.buzz | Direct MP4 | None / app token; 300 per 5 min | Attribute, respect `sensitive` | ~13k raw |
| PeerTube | HLS + MP4 | None; 50 per 10 s per instance | Per-video licence; 56% Unknown | ~130 short; 10-20 on-theme |
| YouTube | Embed only | 100 searches/day + 10k units | IFrame player, 1 autoplay per screen, no caching | ≤5k search candidates; more via channel uploads |
| Twitch clips | Embed only | App token, points bucket | `parent` domain, min 400x300, no mobile autoplay | Thousands, mostly off-theme gaming |
| Odysee / LBRY | Direct (undocumented CDN) | None documented | ToS unreadable | Unmeasured |
| Internet Archive | Direct MP4 | None; 10k sorted cap | Per-item licence | Low hundreds (unmeasured) |
| Reddit | HLS/DASH hotlink | OAuth; **API ends March 2027** | Display-only licence | Zero after shutdown |
| Telegram | Expiring CDN links | Scrape / bot capped at 20 MB | Copyright per post | High volume, high risk |
| X | MP4 variants (historical) | **$0.005 per post read** | Developer Agreement | Not viable (~$5 per 1k posts) |
| TikTok / Instagram | oEmbed HTML only | None / 1,000 per hour | Front-end view only | 0 discovery |
| Dailymotion / Vimeo / Kick / Rumble | Embed or nothing | Scoped or no API | Embed; Rumble forbids scraping | ~0 |

YouTube is the only big platform worth adding, and only through embeds. `search.list` now has its **own bucket of 100 calls per day** (up to 50 results each). Other endpoints share 10,000 units/day. No parameter filters for Shorts: `videoDuration=short` means under four minutes ([quota costs](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/determine_quota_cost); [search.list](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/search/list)). The cheaper route is `playlistItems.list` on hand-picked news channels' upload playlists, then `videos.list` (50 IDs per unit) to keep clips of 60 seconds or less. The rules are strict. You may not download or cache videos, API data must be refreshed within 30 days, the player must be at least 200x200 with nothing overlaid, **only one player may autoplay per screen**, and the Referer header must identify your app ([Developer Policies](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies); [Required Minimum Functionality](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/required-minimum-functionality)). A one-at-a-time reel UI can comply, but it cannot autoplay the preloaded next card.

Reddit would have been the second-best source for The Void's themes (r/CombatFootage, r/PublicFreakout and similar), but it is closing. Reddit announced on 2026-09-30 that **new API access requests close on 2026-10-31, RSS ends on 2026-11-13, and the public Data API ends in March 2027** ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/reddit-to-halt-public-data-api-access-in-march-2027-as-14-000-apps-register/)). X ended its free tier and charges $0.005 per post read, so even 20,000 posts/day costs about $3,000/month ([X API pricing](https://docs.x.com/x-api/getting-started/pricing)). TikTok and Instagram expose only oEmbed, with no discovery API. Instagram forbids storing oEmbed data for anything beyond a front-end view, so you cannot build an index from it ([TikTok embed](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/embed-videos); [Instagram oEmbed](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/oembed)). Telegram's `t.me/s` pages are scraping, with expiring CDN links and per-IP bans, and Bot API downloads are capped at **20 MB** ([Bot FAQ](https://core.telegram.org/bots/faq)). It has plenty of geopolitics footage, but the copyright exposure is worst exactly where the content fits best. Odysee is the interesting unknown. Its `claim_search` API takes `duration "<=60"`, `release_time` and tag filters with no auth ([lbry-sdk api.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lbryio/lbry-sdk/master/docs/api.json)), but the stream URLs are undocumented and its ToS would not load. Run it as a measured experiment, not a pillar. Internet Archive is clean but mostly long and archival ([About search](https://archive.org/help/aboutsearch.htm)).

## Indie "infinite" feeds run on a firehose, SQLite and a cheap labeller

None of the indie video clients researched stores video files. All of them keep metadata plus the origin's media URL and fill their pool with a filtered firehose, a cron crawler over an instance list, or an archive import. The clearest public example is spacecowboy's "For You" and "Videos For You" Bluesky feeds. They run as **one Go binary that reads Jetstream into SQLite**, serving ~72,000 daily users at 15-25 queries per second for **about $30/month**. Ranking uses collaborative filtering on likes ("people who liked the same posts as you"), plus a log of what each user was shown and how they reacted ([AT Protocol blog](https://atproto.com/blog/serving-the-for-you-feed)). Gorse packages the same retrieval mix (popular, recent, user-based, item-based) with a click-through ranker if you would rather not write it yourself ([Gorse](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/gorse-io/gorse@master/README.md)).

For The Void, the heavy step is labelling, and it is cheap. Labelling a thumbnail plus caption costs about **$0.05 per 1,000 clips on Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite batch, and ~$0.06-0.09 per 1,000 on Claude Haiku 5.5 batch** with the system prompt cached. That is my arithmetic from list prices: $0.05 per million input tokens on both, roughly 258-1,300 image tokens per thumbnail ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing); [Gemini image tokens](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-understanding)). Labelling all ~110,000 daily videos would cost $150-300/month, so filter cheaply first by language, duration, aspect ratio, content labels and creator allow/deny lists. Then label about 20,000 clips/day for **~$30-55/month**. Have the labeller return topic, theme match, quality and an adult-content flag.

On ranking, short-video research agrees on the signals: **early skips are negative (most skips happen in the first five seconds), completion and replay are positive**, and raw watch time is biased toward longer clips ([AAAI 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03107v1); [Kuaishou](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13249); [ShareChat](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.14276)). TikTok's own public description adds diversity rules: no two consecutive videos from the same creator or sound, and deliberate exploration outside the user's stated interests ([TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)). A workable indie version pulls 200-500 unseen candidates per request from theme labels, likes-based neighbours and a fresh/popular fallback. It scores them with a hand-tuned formula, applies the diversity rules, and sends 8-10 clips at a time while prefetching 2-3 ahead. No published paper says how big the unseen pool per session must be, so the 200-300 clips per heavy user per day figure is derived, not measured.

The legal line is clear: **play from the origin, never rehost**. In the US, the Ninth Circuit's server test (Perfect 10 v. Amazon, reaffirmed in Hunley v. Instagram in 2023) holds that embedding or inline-linking media hosted elsewhere is not a "display" by you. New York federal courts reject that test (Goldman v. Breitbart, Nicklen v. Mashable), so the protection depends on where you are sued ([Copyright Lately](https://copyrightlately.com/ninth-circuit-affirms-server-test-in-instagram-embedding-case); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_10,_Inc._v._Amazon.com,_Inc.)). In the EU, BestWater (2014) allows embedding of freely available content. VG Bild-Kunst (2021) makes it infringement **if you get around anti-framing or anti-hotlinking measures** ([EDRi](https://edri.org/our-work/cjeu-embedding-copyright-infringement/); [SCL](https://www.scl.org/12210-cjeu-confirms-that-framing-embedded-content-on-a-website-may-require-authorisation-of-the-copyright-holder/)). Hotlinking Bluesky's HLS, PeerTube files and Mastodon MP4s is defensible. Downloading clips into R2 is a reproduction and loses both defences unless you hold a licence or qualify as a DMCA §512 host of user uploads. Streaming bytes through your Worker is a grey zone in between. The one proxy worth running is a thumbnail cache, because R2 egress is free and 100,000 thumbnails cost cents per month ([R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)).

## Conclusion

Rank the sources by how much on-theme, directly playable, legally clean video each adds per dollar. First, **the Bluesky Jetstream video tail plus 10-15 curated video feeds**: ~97,000 raw and 1-3k curated per day, for a $5-7 VPS. Second, **Mastodon through fedi.buzz and app-token tag timelines**: ~13,000 raw per day, free. Third, **polling a dozen PeerTube news and activism instances, with Sepia for backfill**: only 10-20 fresh on-theme clips per day, but they are the most on-brand and draw on a 196,000-clip short archive. Fourth, **YouTube embeds from chosen news channels**: a few thousand candidates per day, held to the one-autoplay-per-screen rule. Fifth, **an Odysee claim_search experiment**. Together that comes to **~110,000-130,000 raw candidates/day and an estimated 2,000-10,000 labelled, on-theme clips/day**. Monthly cost: VPS $5-10, Cloudflare Workers Paid $5, R2 thumbnails under $1, labelling ~20,000 clips/day $30-55. That is **about $40-70/month**, under the $100 cap.

The million figure misses the point. TikTok's depth comes from its own uploaders, and no open API sells that. What makes a feed feel endless is ranking and skipping what the user has already seen across a 30-90 day window. Two unknowns should be measured before anyone builds on these numbers: a full 24-hour Jetstream run, because every per-day Bluesky and fedi.buzz figure here comes from a single 60-90 second window, and the share of clips that pass the theme and safety filters. Bluesky's moderation duties (report, block, propagate deletions) are the price of the best source on the list, so build them before you launch.
